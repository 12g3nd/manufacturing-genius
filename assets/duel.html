Press a row to play one round. The higher final score wins; a tie splits the prize.

Let’s put you in the lead for a moment. You’re player A, there’s one decision left, and staying safe sounds reasonable enough. Your rival, however, gets the same choice. You both decide at the same time, so there’s no waiting to see what they do first. Pick safe or risky, then change the rival’s rule and try again. You might find that protecting a lead has quite a bit to do with the person behind you.

Here are the rules. Playing safe adds nothing. Playing risky adds either +10 or −10 on a coin flip. To begin with, if you both go risky, you get the same coin flip. Whoever finishes higher takes the prize, and a tie splits it. Nothing else counts: finishing on 115 instead of 106 gets you nothing extra.
